Build a nine

You've bought a hilly piece of land. Design a 9-hole course that's fun, fair, walkable and doesn't wreck the woods. It also has to come in under budget.

  1. Route a hole (R): click the tee, click dogleg turns, then click the last point again to place the green. Fairway, tee and green are shaped for you.
  2. Shape it with brushes: add bunkers and ponds where the landing zones are, widen fairways, clear trees, and erase to put it back to nature.
  3. Playtest (P): a scratch and a bogey golfer each play the hole. Watch where their shots go.
  4. Par follows yardage, like the USGA: up to 250 yds is a par 3, up to 470 is a par 4, up to 690 is a par 5.
  5. Good design puts hazards near the shots golfers actually hit, so good players have decisions to make and bogey players can still get round. Start hole 1 and finish hole 9 at the clubhouse, keep green-to-tee walks short, and don't route holes across each other.

Can't build in the creek or the protected wetland. Carrying a shot over them is fine. Anything off the property or into the clubhouse is out of bounds.
